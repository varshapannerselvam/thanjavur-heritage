TAMIL NADU · CHOLA CAPITAL

The city that carved its name in granite and gold.

Thanjavur has spent a thousand years turning stone into scripture, brass into deity, and silk thread into gold-leaf paintings. This is a small guide to the temple town's history, sights, and living arts.

A thousand years, still standing

The Brihadeeswarar Temple has not needed a single day of scaffolding repair since the Chola king Rajaraja I finished it in 1010 CE.

Three reasons to know this city

An empire's capital

Thanjavur was the seat of the Chola empire at its height — the dynasty that built temples across South and Southeast Asia and ran a navy that reached Sumatra.

An art form of its own

Thanjavur painting — gold foil, semi-precious stones, and rich colour set on wood — is a GI-tagged craft still practised by the same families that started it centuries ago.

A music and dance capital

The Thanjavur Quartet shaped Bharatanatyam into the form performed worldwide today, and the city's veena-makers still hand-carve the instrument from a single jackfruit log.
