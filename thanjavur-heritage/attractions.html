PLACES TO VISIT

Four places worth an unhurried afternoon

Thanjavur rewards slow visits — most of its landmarks sit within a few kilometres of each other.

Brihadeeswarar Temple

The city's centrepiece — a granite temple to Shiva with a 66-metre vimana tower, built without mortar, and topped by a single 80-ton capstone. Go at sunrise to see the tower's shadow, which local legend says never falls on the ground.

Thanjavur Maratha Palace

Home to the Saraswathi Mahal Library — one of Asia's oldest libraries, holding over 49,000 manuscripts, including rare palm-leaf texts on medicine and astronomy collected by the Maratha kings.

Saraswathi Mahal Library

A working research library since the Nayak period, later expanded by Serfoji II, who added European scientific texts to a collection that already spanned Sanskrit, Tamil, Marathi, and Telugu manuscripts.

Schwartz Church

Built in 1779 by order of a Thanjavur king in honour of a Danish missionary, this small church is one of the more unusual monuments in a city otherwise defined by Hindu temple architecture.

Planning a visit

Best time

November to February, when daytime temperatures stay closer to 25–30°C instead of the delta's summer heat.

Getting around

Most landmarks are walkable from the temple complex; auto-rickshaws cover longer distances cheaply.

Local tip

Ask a vendor near the temple for genuine Thanjavur betel leaves — the region is known for a distinct variety grown in the delta.
