ARTS & CRAFT

Where gold leaf, dance, and music still come from one street

Thanjavur didn't just host these art forms — it standardised many of them into the versions practised worldwide today.

Thanjavur painting

A style built on wood panels using real gold foil, glass beads, and semi-precious stones set in relief. It carries a Geographical Indication tag, and the best pieces still come from a handful of family workshops near the palace.

Bharatanatyam

The four Thanjavur brothers — remembered as the Thanjavur Quartet — codified the repertoire and technique that turned a temple dance tradition into the structured art form performed on stages worldwide.

The Thanjavur veena

Carved from a single block of jackfruit wood, the Thanjavur veena is the standard instrument of Carnatic music, and its makers here also hold a GI tag for the craft.

Thanjavur plate work

A repoussé metalworking craft — silver and brass sheets embossed over wood — used for temple decor and ceremonial plates, still practised by artisan families in the old town.

A city that still practises what it perfected

Unlike museum crafts, all four of these traditions are still actively taught and sold in Thanjavur today — from painting workshops that offer short courses to veena-makers who take custom orders.
