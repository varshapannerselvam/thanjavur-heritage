HISTORY

From a river town to an empire's capital

Thanjavur sits on the fertile delta of the Kaveri river — the reason every dynasty that controlled it also controlled the region's rice bowl.

Timeline

9th century

The Medieval Cholas rise from a regional power to rulers of the Kaveri delta, choosing Thanjavur as their capital.

1010 CE

Rajaraja Chola I completes the Brihadeeswarar Temple, funded partly by tribute from his campaigns in Sri Lanka and the Deccan.

1674

The Bhonsle (Maratha) dynasty takes control of Thanjavur, and rulers like Serfoji II later turn the palace into a centre for science, medicine, and the arts.

1799

Thanjavur comes under British administration as a princely state, while the Saraswathi Mahal Library keeps growing under royal patronage.

1987

UNESCO inscribes the Brihadeeswarar Temple as a World Heritage Site, part of the "Great Living Chola Temples" group.

Why the Cholas built like this

Chola temple building wasn't only devotion — it was administration. Grants inscribed on temple walls recorded land ownership, tax exemptions, and village assemblies, making temples the empire's record office as much as its place of worship.

Engineering without modern tools

The Brihadeeswarar Temple's 80-ton capstone was reportedly hauled up a four-mile earthen ramp — a method still debated by historians and engineers today.

A living document in stone

Over 800 inscriptions on the temple's base record donations, dancers' names, and the wages paid to temple musicians — a rare window into Chola-era daily life.
